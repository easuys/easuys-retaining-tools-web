Verification & validation

Retaining wall analysis

Comparison evidence, scope and known limitations for the EA Suys retaining tool.

Validation status 20 October 2026

Purpose and scope

What the tool computes

The tool analyses a flexible retaining wall through construction stages. It reports wall displacement, bending moment and shear along the wall, together with support forces for the entered geometry, soil, water, loads and stage conditions.

It is intended as a design aid for geotechnical and structural engineers. It does not replace engineering judgement, complete input checks or independent review of the design.

Method in brief

Staged beam and soil-spring model

The wall is modelled as a beam supported by elastoplastic soil springs. The calculation advances through excavation and support stages; spring response is carried between stages as geometry, water levels and loads change. The reported response depends on the entered soil properties, earth-pressure assumptions and construction sequence.

Comparison approach

Independent reference calculations

Results were compared with independent reference calculations for public examples and published manual tutorials. Comparisons cover wall displacement, bending moment, shear, support forces and selected effective stresses where those quantities are reported. For the SPW 2006 examples, the response was compared at the reference nodes.

These comparisons cover the cases and quantities listed below. They do not establish performance for every soil profile, loading condition, construction sequence or design procedure.

Staging convention

The larger differences in Ex1 step 2 and Ex3 steps 1–2 and 4–5 occur when vertical effective stress changes at springs that have already moved. One calculation convention retains the spring displacement offset and recomputes pressure with the updated neutral stress and stiffness. The engine carries pressure forward and shifts it by the change in neutral pressure, a convention also used in the published manual comparison below. These are differences in modelling convention.

In Ex3q, the 20 kPa surcharge was applied earlier, with the preceding excavation stage. Later displacement differences were 0.9–1.9%, moment differences 1.2–1.7%, and the 8 m support force was 264.8 / 259.4 kN/m (reference / engine).

The two editions differ for this stage. The 2023 manual describes a Fine calculation, which evaluates Culmann coefficients at minor nodes five times as often as the Coarse setting described in the 9.1 manual. This stage uses a 20 kPa strip from 0 to 2 m and a 50 kN/m mooring force at the wall top, close to the wall where the manual says calculation settings can matter most. The engine evaluates coefficients at the nodes of its own discretisation, yet its results are closer to the 9.1 values. The reason for the difference cannot be determined from the manuals alone.

The soil friction angles in this example range from 17° to 35°, exceeding the 15° range noted in the 2023 manual for straight Culmann slip surfaces. The engine also reports this range warning, so Tutorial 4 is outside the stated intended range of that method.

Known limitations and open points

Limits of the current comparison

  • The comparison evidence is limited to the cases and outputs shown here; broader benchmark coverage is incomplete.
  • The public request limit is 50 soil layers per profile.
  • In SPW 2006 Ex3 steps 1–2, a weightless submerged layer on the excavated side is not reproduced exactly: the engine applies a minimum submerged unit weight of 0.1 kN/m³, corresponding to 0.6 kPa at 8 m.
  • The larger SPW 2006 differences during stage changes reflect the stated spring-staging conventions. They should be considered when interpreting results for stages that change effective stress around an already displaced wall.
  • Tutorial 2 cannot be reproduced from the manual's printed data because its soil profile is shown only as a plot from a CPT file.
  • Tutorials 5, 7–15 are outside this comparison. They cover required length, design checks, anchor stability, combi-walls, additional pore pressures, limited-width loads, drivability and single piles.
  • The Tutorial 4 difference between manual editions and calculation settings remains unresolved; a direct comparison using both settings is needed to isolate its cause.

Use the results with engineering judgement and check that the chosen model and input assumptions suit the project.
