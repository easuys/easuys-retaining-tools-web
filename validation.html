Verification & validation

Retaining wall analysis

Comparison evidence, scope and known limitations for the EA Suys retaining tool.

Validation status 20 October 2026

Purpose and scope

What the tool computes

The tool analyses a flexible retaining wall through construction stages. It reports wall displacement, bending moment and shear along the wall, together with support forces for the entered geometry, soil, water, loads and stage conditions.

It is intended as a design aid for geotechnical and structural engineers. It does not replace engineering judgement, complete input checks or independent review of the design.

Method in brief

Staged beam and soil-spring model

The wall is modelled as a beam supported by elastoplastic soil springs. The calculation advances through excavation and support stages; spring response is carried between stages as geometry, water levels and loads change. The reported response depends on the entered soil properties, earth-pressure assumptions and construction sequence.

Comparison approach

Independent reference calculations

Results were compared with independent reference calculations for the published examples and test problems of the free program SPW 2006. Comparisons cover wall displacement, bending moment, shear, support forces and selected effective stresses where those quantities are reported. For the SPW 2006 examples, the response was compared at the reference nodes.

These comparisons cover the cases and quantities listed below. They do not establish performance for every soil profile, loading condition, construction sequence or design procedure.

Results at a glance

What the comparisons show

  • SPW 2006, same staging convention: the largest node-by-node difference stays within 2 % for displacement and 4.5 % for bending moment; the analytical checks of Test1 (0.1980 m against 0.200 m) and Test3 (40.15 kN/m against 40.08 kN/m) are reproduced.
  • Larger differences, shown as they are: SPW 2006 Ex1 step 2 (moment 33 %, on a small peak of 2.5 kNm/m), Ex3 steps 1–2 (about 10 %) and Ex3 steps 4–5 (moment 8 %; strut force at 8 m 188.2 kN/m against 234.1 kN/m, +24 %). They come from a different staging convention; a variant that avoids it (Ex3q) agrees within 2 %.

Why some stages differ more: staging convention

The larger differences all occur in a stage that changes the vertical effective stress at soil springs that have already moved:

  • Ex1 step 2: the second layer is excavated on the loaded side. The peak moment is small (2.5 kNm/m), so a difference of 0.83 kNm/m reads as 33 %; the displacement differs by 0.45 mm (5.8 %).
  • Ex3 steps 1–2: the top force displaces the wall before the weight on the excavated side changes.
  • Ex3 steps 4–5: a 20 kPa surcharge is placed on the excavation bottom next to the displaced wall. In step 5 the strut force at 8 m is 188.2 kN/m in the reference and 234.1 kN/m in the engine (+24 %), and the peak moment differs by 8 %. Step 5 also has unequal water levels, for which the engine uses a water unit weight of 9.81 kN/m³ against 10 kN/m³ in the reference input.

The reference program keeps the displacement offset of each spring and re-evaluates the pressure with the new neutral stress and stiffness. The engine carries the pressure forward and shifts it by the change in neutral pressure. Both are modelling conventions, not errors.

Check of this explanation: variant Ex3q applies the same 20 kPa surcharge one stage earlier, together with the step 3 excavation; only that input changes. The later steps then agree like the single-stage cases: displacement within 0.9–1.9 %, moment within 1.2–1.7 %, and strut force at 8 m 264.8 / 259.4 kN/m (2 %), against 8 % in moment and 24 % in strut force in Ex3.

Known limitations and open points

Limits of the current comparison

  • The comparison evidence is limited to the cases and outputs shown here; broader benchmark coverage is incomplete.
  • The public request limit is 50 soil layers per profile.
  • In SPW 2006 Ex3 steps 1–2, a weightless submerged layer on the excavated side is not reproduced exactly: the engine applies a minimum submerged unit weight of 0.1 kN/m³, corresponding to 0.6 kPa at 8 m.
  • The larger SPW 2006 differences during stage changes reflect the stated spring-staging conventions. They should be considered when interpreting results for stages that change effective stress around an already displaced wall.

Use the results with engineering judgement and check that the chosen model and input assumptions suit the project.
